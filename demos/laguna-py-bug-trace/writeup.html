TL;DR

Poolside's Laguna-XS.2 lands 12th of 29 models overall (0.790 mean score), but its real story is an unusual strength on the hardest tasks: it ranks 2nd on Level 3 (0.867) while sitting at only 25th-percentile on Level 1 — the opposite of most competitors. Meanwhile, the benchmark's difficulty levels are so scrambled that 13 of 28 flagged anomalies are inversions where models score better on harder problems, suggesting L2 and L3 test meaningfully different skills rather than a clean difficulty gradient.

The setup

We swept 29 models across py-bug-trace, a benchmark with three difficulty levels (L1–L3, 5 tasks each) that asks models to predict the exact output of subtly broken Python snippets (L1–L2) or fix buggy code (L3). Each cell was scored 0–1 across multiple rollouts. The target model is Laguna-XS.2 (poolside/laguna-xs.2); everything else serves as comparison.

The story across levels

Level 1 (classic Python gotchas): Laguna-XS.2 scored 0.771, ranking 3rd in its cohort but only 25th-percentile across the full leaderboard.

Level 2 (concurrency and asyncio): The asyncio-yield-before-finish task had a mean score of just 0.518. Laguna-XS.2 was one of only three models that solved it. Its 0.733 score placed it 4th (53rd percentile).

Level 3 (code-fix tasks): Laguna-XS.2 jumped to 0.867, rank 2, beaten only by Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (0.933).

Surprises and anomalies

Difficulty levels don't behave like a ladder. Thirteen anomalies are inverted — models doing better on nominally harder levels. L2's concurrency tasks are a different kind of hard: thread-race-counter tripped up GPT-5.3-Codex, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5, and Claude-Opus-4.6, while those same models nailed L3 code-fix tasks.

How the target did

Laguna-XS.2's profile is distinctive: weak-easy, strong-hard. Its L1→L3 trajectory (0.771 → 0.733 → 0.867) runs counter to the field's typical decay. Sibling Laguna-M.1 (rank 17, 0.733 overall) showed the opposite pattern — stronger on L2 (0.867) but weaker on L3 (0.600).
